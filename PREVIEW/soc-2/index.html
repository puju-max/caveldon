Type I vs Type II

Two reports, two different questions.

Type I asks “are the controls designed properly?” Type II asks “did they actually work, every day, for months?”

  1. Weeks

    Readiness

    Assess gaps, improve cloud and delivery controls, and begin collecting appropriate evidence.

  2. One date

    Type I report

    The auditor checks your controls are designed properly at a single point in time.

  3. Usually 3–12 months

    Observation window

    Your controls run for real. Access reviews happen, logs are kept, backups are tested, changes are reviewed.

  4. After the window

    Type II report

    The auditor samples evidence from the whole window. This is the report most enterprise buyers want.

Timelines vary with your starting point, your auditor and your buyers. The Cloud Readiness Review gives you a realistic plan before you commit to dates.

SOC 2 questions

Plain answers to common questions.

Have a question that isn’t here? Ask it on the call.

What is SOC 2, in plain English?

SOC 2 is a report from an independent CPA firm that says how well a service company protects its customers’ data. It’s based on the AICPA’s Trust Services Criteria. Security is always included; availability, confidentiality, processing integrity and privacy are optional.

What’s the difference between SOC 2 Type I and Type II?

Type I checks that your controls are designed properly at a single point in time. Type II checks that they worked consistently over an observation period, usually 3 to 12 months. Many startups get a Type I first to unblock deals, then move to Type II.

How long does it take to get SOC 2-ready on AWS?

It depends on where you start. A team with single sign-on, infrastructure as code and reviewed deployments might need a few weeks of fixes. A team with shared admin logins and manual deploys needs longer. The readiness review gives you a realistic plan before you commit to an audit date.

Which AWS changes come up most often?

Single sign-on with MFA instead of shared IAM users, an organization-wide CloudTrail with long retention, AWS Config and GuardDuty turned on, encryption on databases and storage, tested backups, separate production and staging accounts, and code review required before anything reaches production.

Do compliance platforms replace this work?

No. Platforms like Vanta, Drata and Secureframe connect to AWS, test your settings and collect evidence. When a test fails, an engineer has to change the infrastructure. We do those changes, in code, so they stay fixed.

We sell to hospitals or banks. What changes?

Their security reviews go further than SOC 2. Healthcare buyers may ask about HIPAA safeguards and a business associate agreement; financial buyers may ask about PCI DSS, or DORA if they’re in the EU. The engineering foundation is the same, with stricter access, logging and data controls on top.
