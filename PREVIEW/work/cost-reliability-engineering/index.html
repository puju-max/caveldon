Reliability & cost · AWS and Azure

Make infrastructure efficient without making it fragile.

A practical measurement-first framework for balancing cloud spend, performance, availability and the time teams spend operating systems.

Reference design · not a client project

The problem

Why this matters.

Cloud bills are growing, but reducing resources blindly could introduce slowdowns or outages. You need a responsible way to decide.

The situation

A software team wants better cost allocation and utilization while preserving acceptable latency, recovery and delivery experience.

The goal

A proposed optimization loop with clear workload baselines, change hypotheses and verification criteria.

Technical scope
AWS Cost Explorer / Azure Cost ManagementCloudWatch / Azure MonitorTagging / allocationCapacity reviewsSLOs & recovery tests

The approach

Four design principles.

01

Measure the whole service

Combine cloud charges with utilization, latency, errors, availability and the human effort needed to operate it.

02

Allocate costs meaningfully

Organize costs by environment, workload or business function before comparing changes.

03

Change one hypothesis at a time

Right-size, schedule, scale or redesign only with explicit guardrails and rollback options.

04

Verify in actual conditions

Assess results over representative load patterns, not a quiet hour or a marketing estimate.

The judgement calls

Trade-offs, not just tools.

Every choice has a cost. Here is what each decision buys, and what we’d check before committing to it.

01

Right-size after observing demand

Why it can fit

Sizing decisions should follow workload behavior, p95 latency, memory headroom and known peaks.

What we’d check

Over-aggressive sizing may lower cost but degrade resilience and performance.

02

Evaluate commitments after understanding usage

Why it can fit

Savings Plans and Azure reservations suit some stable resource patterns.

What we’d check

They introduce commitments and should not be purchased on speculative baselines.

03

Preserve recovery targets as constraints

Why it can fit

Backups, redundant capacity and recovery options may be economically justified.

What we’d check

The lowest infrastructure bill is not necessarily the lowest business risk.

Proving it

How we’d validate it.

This is the test plan, not a record of tests already run.

Evidence

Baseline

Tagged spend, utilization trends, p95 latency, error rates and capacity assumptions.

Evidence

Change record

The proposed adjustment, expected benefit, operating risks and rollback criteria.

Evidence

Verification

Post-change performance, spend, reliability and operator observations compared with baseline.

  1. Establish a representative measurement period and identify missing allocation tags.
  2. Document service-level expectations and the behavior that must not deteriorate.
  3. Run one controlled optimization experiment with rollback instructions.
  4. Review actual spend and service behavior over a suitable period; report both wins and regressions.
What this is and isn’t: A decision and measurement framework only. No cloud bill reduction percentage, SLA improvement, or production experiment is claimed.

Next milestone: The next proof milestone is a synthetic workload lab with published measurement methods, reproducible load profiles and documented trade-offs.
