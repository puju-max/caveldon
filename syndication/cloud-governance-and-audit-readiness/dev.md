> <strong>Short answer:</strong> Governance sets decision rights and guardrails. Compliance concerns applicable obligations and control requirements. Audit assesses controls and supporting evidence against a defined scope. They are related, but they are not interchangeable.

## Governance: define how cloud decisions are made

Engineering governance can include account structure, least-privilege access, tagging standards, approved deployment paths, configuration policies, backup expectations, change approvals and ownership. These are meaningful whether or not a formal audit is imminent.

## Compliance: identify the actual requirements

Requirements depend on the organization, industry, jurisdictions, contractual obligations and specific frameworks. A cloud provider’s certifications do not automatically make an application or customer organization compliant.

## Audit readiness: make evidence available

Engineering teams can support evidence collection and control implementation. Examples include access review records, configuration snapshots, logging policies, change histories, infrastructure review artifacts, backup test records and remediation tracking.

## What engineering support can responsibly offer

- Review and improve cloud technical controls.
- Map control implementation to supplied requirements in collaboration with compliance owners.
- Assist with evidence collection and remediation workflows.
- Document limitations, unresolved gaps, and shared responsibilities.

**Boundary:** Technical audit-readiness support is not the same as providing an independent audit opinion, legal advice, or certification. Those require the appropriate qualified providers and clearly defined engagements.
