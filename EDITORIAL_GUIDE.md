# CAVELDON Insights — Editorial Standard

## The promise

Write technical content that helps an engineering leader make a better decision. Be precise, calm, useful and transparent about uncertainty. Our writing should demonstrate engineering judgment rather than advertise superiority.

## An article should answer five questions

1. What problem is the reader experiencing?
2. What decision needs to be made?
3. What are the realistic technical options and trade-offs?
4. What evidence or measurements would validate the choice?
5. What can the reader do next?

## Evidence and publishing review

- Cite primary documentation for technical features or policy requirements, and link to the relevant source with its publication or review date when helpful.
- Distinguish legal obligations, industry standards, cloud-provider configurations, and CAVELDON's technical support role.
- Do not use phrases such as "HIPAA certified by AWS" or "GDPR requires all data to stay in the EU"; explain scope and limitations.
- Never imply confidential employer work is a CAVELDON client engagement. Recreate a stand-alone laboratory example or get publication permission.
- No fictional customer names, case-study metrics, testimonials, certifications, or guaranteed compliance outcomes.
- Include meaningful alternative approaches in AWS/Azure/cloud comparisons rather than assuming one provider is categorically better.
- Check architecture figures against the article, use descriptive image alt text, and optimize images before publication.
- Keep `draft: true` until editorial, legal/security and technical reviews are complete.

## Channel distribution

**CAVELDON Insights** is the primary source. Publish the full, reviewed article there first. Write a shorter LinkedIn post with one useful takeaway and a link. For Medium, consider a condensed adaptation with a prominent original-source link; use canonical controls only where the platform supports them, and do not assume they guarantee search ranking.

## Example publishing cadence

Aim for one deeply useful article instead of several shallow posts. Quality, original observations, and technical verification matter more than volume.
